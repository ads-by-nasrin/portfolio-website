How I work

Anyone can switch on a campaign. The difference is in the order of thinking: business first, ads second.

1

Start with the business

Before touching the account, I need to understand the economics behind it:

  • Average job or treatment value, and your margin
  • How many enquiries become paying customers
  • What you can afford to pay for a booked customer
  • Your budget and the area you actually serve
2

Audit before action

I review the account, the website and the tracking as they are today. Findings are separated into confirmed issues, evidence-based opportunities, and things that need more data — so we fix what's real first, in order of impact.

3

Relevance at every step

Keyword, ad and landing page must say the same thing to the same person. When someone searches with a specific problem, the ad speaks to that problem and the page continues the conversation — that's what turns clicks into enquiries.

4

Control the waste, then scale

Ongoing search-term reviews, negative keywords and budget checks cut spend that produces nothing. What's left goes behind the services, searches and areas that bring qualified leads — and reporting shows you exactly that.

Booked appointments, not clicks: how I judge a Google Ads campaign

By Nasrin Akther · Google Ads for dental clinics & local businesses

Most Google Ads reports lead with the same numbers: impressions, clicks, click-through rate, cost per click. They look impressive in a dashboard. But none of them pay a clinic's rent. What pays is a patient in the chair, a booked job, a customer who actually bought something. So that is where I start — and it changes almost every decision in a campaign.

A click is not a customer

A click only means someone was curious enough to tap an ad. They might be comparing prices with no intention of booking. They might be a student researching a project, a competitor checking your ads, or someone in a completely different area who will never visit your business. If a campaign is judged on clicks, all of those people count as success. Judged on booked appointments and qualified enquiries, they don't.

What "qualified" actually means

A qualified lead is an enquiry from someone who could realistically become a customer: they are in the area you serve, they want a service you actually offer, and they are looking to book or buy — not just browsing. For a dental clinic, that is the difference between someone searching for a specific treatment they are ready to book, and someone idly reading about dentistry. Both can click your ad. Only one of them matters.

How this changes the campaign

  • Keywords are chosen by intent and commercial value, not by search volume. A phrase with fewer searches but clear booking intent is worth more than a popular, vague one.
  • Negative keywords become a weekly habit. Every search that can never become a customer — free, DIY, jobs, courses, the wrong location — is budget taken away from people who can.
  • Ad copy speaks to the searcher's actual problem, so the right person clicks and the wrong person doesn't. A click you never pay for is as valuable as a good one.
  • Bidding is set against real economics — what a job or treatment is worth, your margin, and how many enquiries become customers — not against an arbitrary cost-per-click target.
  • Tracking has to separate a genuine enquiry from a casual interaction. If calls, form submissions and bookings are not recorded properly, every other decision is guesswork, so I audit the measurement before I trust the numbers.

The question to ask any ads manager

Before you hire anyone — including me — ask one question: "How will I know whether this is bringing me customers, not just traffic?" A good answer talks about enquiries, bookings and lead quality, and about checking those numbers against what your team actually receives. A vague answer talks about impressions and reach. The difference between those two answers is the difference between an expense and an investment.

That is the standard I hold my own work to: business goals first, ads second, and reporting that connects spend to the outcomes a business owner actually cares about.
