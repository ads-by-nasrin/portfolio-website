Booked appointments, not clicks: how I judge a Google Ads campaign

By Nasrin Akther · Google Ads for dental clinics & local businesses

Most Google Ads reports lead with the same numbers: impressions, clicks, click-through rate, cost per click. They look impressive in a dashboard. But none of them pay a clinic's rent. What pays is a patient in the chair, a booked job, a customer who actually bought something. So that is where I start — and it changes almost every decision in a campaign.

A click is not a customer

A click only means someone was curious enough to tap an ad. They might be comparing prices with no intention of booking. They might be a student researching a project, a competitor checking your ads, or someone in a completely different area who will never visit your business. If a campaign is judged on clicks, all of those people count as success. Judged on booked appointments and qualified enquiries, they don't.

What "qualified" actually means

A qualified lead is an enquiry from someone who could realistically become a customer: they are in the area you serve, they want a service you actually offer, and they are looking to book or buy — not just browsing. For a dental clinic, that is the difference between someone searching for a specific treatment they are ready to book, and someone idly reading about dentistry. Both can click your ad. Only one of them matters.

How this changes the campaign

  • Keywords are chosen by intent and commercial value, not by search volume. A phrase with fewer searches but clear booking intent is worth more than a popular, vague one.
  • Negative keywords become a weekly habit. Every search that can never become a customer — free, DIY, jobs, courses, the wrong location — is budget taken away from people who can.
  • Ad copy speaks to the searcher's actual problem, so the right person clicks and the wrong person doesn't. A click you never pay for is as valuable as a good one.
  • Bidding is set against real economics — what a job or treatment is worth, your margin, and how many enquiries become customers — not against an arbitrary cost-per-click target.
  • Tracking has to separate a genuine enquiry from a casual interaction. If calls, form submissions and bookings are not recorded properly, every other decision is guesswork, so I audit the measurement before I trust the numbers.

The question to ask any ads manager

Before you hire anyone — including me — ask one question: "How will I know whether this is bringing me customers, not just traffic?" A good answer talks about enquiries, bookings and lead quality, and about checking those numbers against what your team actually receives. A vague answer talks about impressions and reach. The difference between those two answers is the difference between an expense and an investment.

That is the standard I hold my own work to: business goals first, ads second, and reporting that connects spend to the outcomes a business owner actually cares about.

The Google Ads Hidden Traps Guide

14 default settings that quietly waste your ad budget — and the simple fix for each one.

By Nasrin Akther, Google Ads Specialist and Performance Marketing Strategist, Ads by Nasrin · 5 November 2026

Google Ads opens with settings already chosen for you. Most of them help Google spend your budget faster. They do not always help you get booked appointments. This guide shows you what to check before that costs you money.

  • Written in plain English. No jargon, no long theory.
  • Dental clinic examples, so you can picture your own ads.
  • A one-page checklist at the end. Print it and tick as you go.

Defaults are not set for your clinic

When you build a new campaign, Google pre-ticks a lot of boxes. Each one looks harmless. Together, they can send your ads to the wrong people, at the wrong time, for the wrong searches. You do not need a big budget to fix this. You need to check the settings, one by one. Here are the first three.

1

Search and Display mixed in one campaign

A person typing "emergency dentist near me" is ready to book. A person scrolling a random website is not. When both networks sit in one campaign, they share one budget and the cheap Display clicks can eat it.

Fix: Keep Search and Display in separate campaigns. For most clinics, start with Search only.

2

Ads showing to people outside your area

Targeting Dublin does not mean only people in Dublin see your ad. The loose location setting also shows ads to people who are just interested in that area, including people who will never travel to your clinic.

Fix: In Location options, choose Presence: People in or regularly in your targeted locations.

3

Ads running when nobody can answer

If your ad runs at 11 pm and the phone goes to voicemail, you pay for a call that goes nowhere. Wasted clicks at night and on closed days add up fast.

Fix: Set an ad schedule that matches the hours your team can answer and book. If you take real emergencies around the clock, keep a separate emergency campaign for those hours instead.

Smart bidding is only as good as your tracking

These four catch clinics out because nothing looks broken. The campaign runs, clicks come in, and the money still leaks.

4

No proper conversion tracking

If calls, booking forms and appointment requests are not tracked as conversions, Google cannot tell a booked patient from a casual click. It will optimise for the wrong thing.

Fix: Track calls, form submissions and bookings first. Count only real enquiries as primary conversions, not page views.

5

The wrong bidding strategy for your stage

A brand new campaign has no history. Handing it a sales target on day one, or following every prompt to raise bids and budgets, usually means paying more before you have learned anything.

Fix: Start controlled while data is thin. Move to Target CPA or Target ROAS only when you have steady, correctly tracked conversions to learn from.

6

Recommendations switched on automatically

Google shows helpful-looking suggestions, and some accounts apply them on their own. A suggestion can change your keywords, ads or budget without you ever reading it properly.

Fix: Turn off auto-apply. Read each recommendation, keep what fits your goal, and dismiss the rest.

7

Only one ad, left to run itself

One ad means one message tested against every patient. You never find out which headline actually brings calls. Old guides told you to rotate ads forever — that setting is gone now, so testing has to be planned.

Fix: Write ads with varied headlines about the real problem, such as tooth pain or implants. Check asset performance, and replace weak headlines with new ones.

Stop paying for searches and sites you never wanted

Clicks feel like progress. Irrelevant clicks are just a bill. These four decide who actually sees and clicks your ads.

8

Starting with Broad Match only

One broad word like "dentist" or "implants" can mean many things. Someone may want a job, a course, free treatment or a clinic in another country. You pay for all of it.

Fix: Start core campaigns on Phrase and Exact Match, with clear searches like "emergency dentist near me". Test Broad later, only with tracking, negatives and a budget you control.

9

No negative keywords

Without negatives, your ads can show for searches you would never pay for. Think "free", "jobs", "salary", "DIY" and treatments you do not offer.

Fix: Add a starter negative list before launch. Then read the Search Terms report every week and add new waste as negatives.

10

No placement exclusions on Display and video

Your ad can end up on phone games, odd apps and websites that have nothing to do with your clinic. The clicks are cheap because nobody there wants a dentist.

Fix: Review where your ads appeared and exclude poor placements. Keep a shared exclusion list you can use again.

11

No content or brand safety exclusions

Irrelevant is one problem. Sensitive or unsuitable content next to your clinic name is another. It does not suit a trusted local business.

Fix: Set your inventory and content exclusions in account settings, and review the sensitive categories instead of leaving them open.

The right click can still be wasted at the end

Sometimes the keyword and the ad are fine. The loss happens in the area you target, the page the person lands on, and the detail missing from the ad itself.

12

Targeting a whole city instead of your real patients

A full city, or a wide radius around it, covers people who will never drive to you. Your budget gets spread thin across areas that rarely book.

Fix: Start with where your current patients actually come from. Use your call and booking records, postcodes or ZIP codes, and build tighter areas around the places that already send you patients.

13

Sending every click to your homepage

Someone searching for implants lands on a general homepage and has to hunt for the right page. Many will not bother. They go back and click the next clinic.

Fix: Give each main treatment its own page — implants, emergency care, Invisalign — with a clear call button and booking option on that page.

14

Missing or weak ad assets

Assets are the extra parts of your ad: the call button, your location, callouts and structured snippets. A plain ad takes up less space and gives a patient fewer reasons to pick you.

Fix: Add call and location assets, callouts such as "Evening appointments", and snippets that list your real treatments. Only claim what you truly offer.

One honest note. Older checklists you may find online still mention Manual CPC as the safe choice, Enhanced CPC, Accelerated delivery and rotating ads indefinitely. Those options have been removed from Google Ads, so do not go looking for them in your account. The fixes in this guide are the ones that match how the platform works now.

Your one-page Hidden Traps checklist

Before you launch

  • Search and Display are in separate campaigns
  • Location is set to Presence: people in or regularly in your area
  • Ad schedule matches the hours your team can answer
  • Calls, forms and bookings are tracked as conversions
  • Auto-apply for recommendations is off
  • Starter negative keyword list is added

In the first week

  • Bidding fits your stage — no target set on guesswork
  • Core keywords are Phrase and Exact, not Broad only
  • Each treatment sends clicks to its own page, not the homepage
  • Call, location, callout and snippet assets are live

Every week

  • Search Terms report checked, new waste added as negatives
  • Placements and content exclusions reviewed
  • Target area checked against where enquiries really came from
  • Weak ad headlines replaced, strong ones kept
